Business days calculator

Weekends are always skipped. U.S. federal holidays are skipped on their observed dates unless you turn that off.

Business days are weekdays, Monday through Friday, that are not U.S. federal holidays. When counting, the start date is left out and the end date is included, so Monday to the following Monday is 5 business days. When adding, counting begins on the next business day.     Rules used

    • Counting includes the end date and leaves out the start date, so Monday to the following Monday is 5 business days. Tick the start-date box to count both ends.
    • Adding starts the next business day after the start date: 1 business day after a Friday is the following Monday. Adding 0 returns the start date.
    • Holidays are the eleven U.S. federal holidays from this list. When one falls on a Saturday, the Friday before is skipped; when it falls on a Sunday, the Monday after is skipped. That is how federal offices and many employers observe them.
    • Bank, stock market, state, and company holidays can differ from the federal list. Turn holidays off to count weekdays only.

    Frequently asked questions

    Is the start date counted?

    Not by default. A count includes the end date and leaves out the start date, so Monday to the following Monday is 5 business days. Tick “Count the start date too” to count both ends, which makes it 6.

    Which holidays are skipped?

    The eleven U.S. federal holidays set by 5 U.S.C. 6103: New Year’s Day, Martin Luther King Jr. Day, Washington’s Birthday, Memorial Day, Juneteenth, Independence Day, Labor Day, Columbus Day, Veterans Day, Thanksgiving Day, and Christmas Day. Untick the holiday box to count weekdays only.

    What if a holiday falls on a weekend?

    The observed day is skipped instead: a Saturday holiday is observed on the Friday before and a Sunday holiday on the Monday after. For example, July 4, 2026 is a Saturday, so Friday, July 3 is not a business day.

    What date is 10 business days from today?

    Choose “Add or subtract business days,” leave the start date as today, and enter 10. Counting begins with the next business day, so 1 business day after a Friday is the following Monday. Use a negative number to count back.

    Does this match bank or stock market holidays?

    Not exactly. Banks, stock exchanges, states, and employers set their own holiday calendars, which can include or move days differently from the federal list. Company-specific closings and one-off days off are not included.

    Which years are supported?

    Dates from 1971 onward, when the current Monday-holiday rules began. Juneteenth is counted from 2021 and Martin Luther King Jr. Day from 1986, matching when each became a federal holiday.
