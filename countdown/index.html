How the countdown stays accurate

The date and time you enter are read as a wall-clock time in the chosen zone, then converted to one exact moment using the time zone database built into your browser. That database knows when each place switches to or from daylight saving time, so a countdown that crosses a clock change is still exact, even if your own zone changes on a different day.

If you pick a time that happens twice because clocks fall back, the first occurrence is used. If you pick a time that is skipped when clocks spring forward, the countdown uses the moment the clocks jump past it, and a note says so.

The countdown follows your device clock. If that clock is wrong, the countdown will be off by the same amount.

Frequently asked questions

Which time zone does the countdown use?

The one you choose. It starts as your own zone, and any IANA zone your browser knows, such as America/New_York or Europe/London, can be picked. Someone opening your link sees the same moment converted to their own clock.

Does it handle daylight saving time?

Yes. The target is converted to one exact moment using the browser’s time zone data, so a countdown that crosses a clock change stays exact. A time that happens twice when clocks fall back uses the first one, and a time skipped when clocks spring forward uses the moment the clocks jump past it.

How do I share a countdown?

Use “Copy link to this result.” The link holds the event name, date, time, and time zone, and nothing is stored on a server.

What happens after the moment passes?

The display switches to counting the time since the moment.

Why might the countdown be a little off?

It follows your device clock. If that clock is wrong, the countdown is off by the same amount.
